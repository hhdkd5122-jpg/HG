### 1. The question (0:00–0:20)
*(Look at the audience and speak slowly.)*

Picture a 16-year-old. Healthy. Active. And already carrying cholesterol that's quietly too high.
Not high enough for a statin. So today, we tell them: **"Come back later."**
*(pause)*
My question is: **what if they started Citrus bergamot today… and never needed a statin until much later?**

### 2. Heart disease starts young (0:20–0:40)
*(Point to the three arteries in Background.)*

These arteries tell the story: fatty streaks in childhood, early plaque in young adults, advanced plaque by middle age.
Heart disease **starts young and shows up late.** In more than **38,000 people followed for 35 years**, childhood risk factors predicted adult heart attacks and strokes.

### 3. Time is the real risk factor (0:40–1:10)
*(Point to the bar chart.)*

Cholesterol hurts you **every single year.**
*(grey bar)* Five years of statins cut heart attacks and strokes by about **22%** per 1 mmol/L lower LDL.
*(green bar)* But people with **lifelong** lower LDL get about **55% less coronary disease** for the same drop.
**More than double the benefit**, just from starting early. **The earlier you start, the less you need.**

### 4. Find them early (1:10–1:30)
*(Point to Early risk detection.)*

We find these patients with five checks: family history, lipids, Lp(a), genetic testing and metabolic signs.
Monogenic FH goes straight to a statin. But many young patients sit in a **grey zone**: too high to ignore, too low for a statin. That's who this idea is for.

### 5. Why bergamot? (1:30–2:05)
*(Point to the mechanism diagram, then the forest plot.)*

**Citrus bergamot** is a citrus from southern Italy. Two of its compounds may act on **the same enzyme statins block.**
In nine adult trials, it lowered LDL by about **40 mg/dL**, and also lowered triglycerides, blood sugar and blood pressure.
**One fruit, several risk factors.**

### 6. What we still need to prove (2:05–2:35)
*(Point to "Young patients 0" in Who has been studied?)*

But a good scientist tells you what's missing. Every key trial was in adults: **zero in young patients.** The evidence is low certainty, and one trial found no effect.
So bergamot is gentler and less proven than a statin. That's why it fits **before** a statin, not instead of one.
*(Point to How to test the hypothesis.)* The next step is a trial, and the key outcome: **how long until they need a statin?**

### 7. The close (2:35–3:00)
*(Step toward the audience and slow down.)*

We know **when** heart disease starts: **young.**
We know **what** drives it: **cholesterol over time.**
The question is whether a fruit can buy our patients **years.**
**I believe it's worth finding out.**
*(Point to the QR code.)* Every source is right here. Thank you.

## If people ask questions

**"So bergamot replaces statins?"** No. It's for young patients who don't need a statin *yet*. Anyone who meets statin criteria, especially with FH, should take the statin.

**"Proof in young people?"** Not yet. That's why it's a hypothesis, and the next step is a trial.

**"How strong compared with a statin?"** Gentler: about 40 mg/dL, while statins usually lower LDL by 30–50% or more.

**"Where do 22% and 55% come from?"** 22% from the CTT meta-analysis of 26 statin trials (Lancet 2010); 55% from Ference 2012, people with genetically lower LDL from birth.
