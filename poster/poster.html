01

Risk starts in childhood, not at 40

LDL-C damage is cumulative: every year of exposure counts.

Cumulative LDL-C exposure by age (illustrative model)
Conceptual curves (LDL-C × years). Each line assumes a constant LDL-C; the earlier the slope falls, the later the threshold is reached. Ference 2012 & 2017; Jacobs (i3C), NEJM 2022.
Childhood risk → adult eventsRisk factors at 3–19 y predict adult CV events (38,589 followed)
Genes are not destinyHigh polygenic risk, healthy lifestyle: ≈46% fewer coronary events
A screening window existsUniversal lipid screening at 9–11 and 17–21 y (NHLBI)
Most teens don't meet statin criteriaA gap between "advice only" and drug therapy
02

Detect

Five checks that flag a high-risk teenager early.

Genetic & early risk screen · age 15–18
Polygenic risk scoreCAD PRS from a genotyping array
Lp(a), onceGenetically set; measure at least once
Family historyPremature CVD in a 1st-degree relative
Lipids & apoBNon-fasting LDL-C, TG, HDL-C
Metabolic profileWaist, blood pressure, glucose / HOMA-IR, liver fat (MASLD), BMI
Pathway A · proposed
Polygenic or metabolic risk
LDL-C below drug thresholds, no monogenic variant → lifestyle + bergamot, re-check every 6–12 months
Pathway B · guideline care
Monogenic FH / very high LDL
LDLR / APOB / PCSK9 variant → statin as indicated. Bergamot is not a substitute here.

Treat early

Proposed study

Genetic screening + Citrus bergamot at 15–18: fewer risk factors before statins are needed?

1ScreenAges 15–18: PRS, Lp(a), family history, lipids, metabolic profile
2SelectHigh polygenic or metabolic risk; monogenic FH referred to guideline care
3RandomiseBergamot polyphenol fraction vs placebo, both with lifestyle advice
412 monthsDouble-blind, with adherence and safety checks (liver, muscle, growth)
5OutcomesLDL-C / apoB, TG, HOMA-IR, BP, waist, liver fat, carotid IMT
