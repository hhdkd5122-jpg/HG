IThe GeneWhy can't the liver let LDL in?

A 10-year-old with adult-level cholesterol

No symptoms. No warning. Only a variant inherited from one parent, and decades of silent exposure ahead. Risk tracks LDL-C × years: high LDL-C from birth means disease decades earlier.

No working receptor allele → little statin effect. Statin efficacy depends on the FH genotype.

FIG 4Same dose, different patients

Drug-handling genes (SLCO1B1, ABCG2, CYP2C9) set how much statin reaches blood and muscle; drug-target genes (LDLR, APOB, PCSK9) set how far LDL-C can fall. Adherence, interacting drugs, thyroid or kidney disease and diet add to both. Handling genes mainly drive toxicity; target genes mainly drive efficacy.

Proposed research question

Could genetically guided treatment combined with dietary and nutraceutical interventions improve LDL-C control in young patients with familial hypercholesterolemia?

Personalised lipid management in young patients with FH: from pharmacogenomics to dietary and nutraceutical interventions.

Take-home
  1. FH genotype predicts how well statins can work.
  2. SLCO1B1 · ABCG2 · CYP2C9 predict how safely.
  3. Few gates left → choose a receptor-independent route.
